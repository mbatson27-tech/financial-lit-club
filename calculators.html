Dividend Yield

Dividend Yield: —

Formula & Example

Dividend Yield = Annualized Dividend ÷ Share Price

McDonald's example: you buy the stock at $100/share. It pays a quarterly dividend of $0.50, so the annualized dividend is $0.50 × 4 = $2.00.

$2.00 ÷ $100 = 2.0%

Price Earnings (P/E) Ratio

P/E Ratio: —

Formula & Example

P/E Ratio = Share Price ÷ EPS

The historical average P/E for S&P 500 companies is roughly 19 (since 1971) — meaning investors pay about $19 for every $1 of earnings.

$38 ÷ $2 = 19x

Earnings Per Share (EPS)

EPS: —

Formula & Example

EPS = Net Earnings ÷ Shares Outstanding

A company earns $10,000,000 for the year and has 5,000,000 shares outstanding.

$10,000,000 ÷ 5,000,000 = $2.00 per share

Market Capitalization

Market Cap: —

Formula & Example

Market Cap = Share Price × Shares Outstanding

A company trades at $100/share and has 5,000,000 shares outstanding.

$100 × 5,000,000 = $500,000,000

Interest Coverage Ratio

Interest Coverage: —

Formula & Example

Interest Coverage = EBITDA ÷ Interest Expense

A ratio of 4x or higher is good to very good; above 8–10x is considered spectacular.

$40,000,000 ÷ $5,000,000 = 8.0x (spectacular)
